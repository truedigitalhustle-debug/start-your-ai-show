That's the part most people miss. Anyone can generate a cool five-second shot. What almost nobody does is make the same character walk into a second video, wearing the same coat, carrying the same object, in the same world — so that the two videos make each other better.

That's the whole game, and it's the reason this page exists. Once you can hold a character and a world across episodes, you don't have a video. You have a show.

The method

How to build a series, not a clip

This is the framework behind both episodes. It is genre-agnostic — swap horror for anything you like and the structure holds.

Lock your cast as images first.Before you generate a single second of video, make a reference sheet for every character, creature and location. That sheet is your identity lock. Text descriptions drift; pictures don't.
Give your character three objects.Mine has a coat, a chain and a bone whistle. Objects are how an audience recognises someone in a new shot, and they give you free story later when you show where each one came from.
Write in 20 to 30 second clips, then stitch.Every model has a clip ceiling. Plan your episode as four chunks with hard cuts between them, generate each one, join them in CapCut. Nobody watching can tell.
Chain the clips with the last few seconds.Trim the previous clip down to its final 2–3 seconds and feed that in as the continuity reference. Feed in the whole clip and the model will splice random earlier shots into your new one.
Make episode two answer a question episode one asked.My first video ended with a man whistling and something huge answering. Episode two is where that thing came from. That single link doubled the value of a video I'd already posted.
Plant one detail only rewatchers will catch.There's a broken oar tied to the giant's hip in both episodes. Nobody clocks it first time. The people who do go back and watch everything again, and then they tell other people.
Post it before it's perfect.My first episode has flaws I can list from memory. It outperformed everything else on the page. Finish beats flawless, every single time.

What it cost me to learn

Nine things that will save you hours

These are all mistakes I actually made on these two episodes. Every one of them cost me generations.

Set your aspect ratio before every single generation. It reverts to 16:9 each time. Three of my clips came out landscape because I set it once at the start of a session and assumed it stuck.
Vertical isn't just a format, it's a creative upgrade. My giant was impressive in 16:9 and oppressive in 9:16. Tall subject, tall frame. Regenerate rather than crop.
Never describe a huge creature with abstract sizes. "As tall as a two-story house" gets ignored. Compare it to objects in the shot — paw wider than that door, shoulder above that roof — and repeat the comparison every time you mention it.
Don't put a giant creature in an eye-level frame with a whole human. The model will shrink the creature to make both fit. Reveal it in pieces, and keep a human-scale object at the bottom of frame instead.
If a character has to earn an object, remove it from the reference image. My character wears a whistle in his sheet. In the prequel he hadn't made it yet, and no amount of "he carries no whistle" worked — the model kept restoring what it could see. I generated a second "before" sheet with it removed and the problem vanished instantly.
Re-tag every character on every mention. Write "the hunter (@Image2)" every time, not just the first. Otherwise the model swaps in whoever last filled a similar role.
Trim your continuity clip to its last 2–3 seconds. Feed in the whole previous clip and the model will splice a random earlier shot into the middle of your new one. I have watched it happen.
Use a reference image for locations, not a paragraph. It locks the setting far harder than prose, and it sidesteps the content filter entirely — which matters, because a written location description is what got one of my clips refused three times.
When a prompt gets refused, don't rewrite it on a hunch. Submit one block at a time until you find the one that fails. I burned three attempts guessing and found it in one pass by testing. Bonus: adding a reassuring line like "nobody is harmed here" makes it worse, because the filter reads the words, not the logic.
